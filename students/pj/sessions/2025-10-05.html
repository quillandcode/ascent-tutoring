PJ's Skill Ladder Session

Tutoring Session Report
🧩 Session Overview

We started today's session with a mental math warm-up, setting the tone for focus and quick thinking. PJ jumped right in, showing impressive recall and accuracy—he completed a mixed set of operations in just 1 minute and 25 seconds, a clear improvement from previous sessions!

From there, we launched into what I call a "Skill Ladder" lesson—a step-by-step build-up toward mastering addition and subtraction with rational numbers. Each rung of the ladder strengthened a key concept needed for the next.

🪜 The Skill Ladder
1
Integers vs. Rational Numbers
We defined both and discussed what makes rational numbers unique.
2
Number Line Traversal
We practiced using movement on the number line to visualize adding and subtracting positive and negative values.
3
Mixed Sign Math
PJ worked on understanding how signs affect the outcome of operations.
4
Finding Least Common Multiples (LCMs)
We explored how LCMs form the foundation for fraction arithmetic.
5
Converting to Like Denominators
Using LCMs, PJ learned to transform fractions to have matching denominators—a key step before adding or subtracting.
6
Applying It All
We put everything together to perform full rational number addition and subtraction problems.
🌟 What We Celebrated — PJ's Strengths

Mental Math Master!

PJ's quick calculation skills are shining. He's developed strong numerical fluency, and it shows.

Integer Confidence!

He handled integer operations like a pro—adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing with positive and negative numbers. PJ even caught and corrected one of his own sign errors, showing he's thinking critically rather than memorizing steps.

🔧 Where We're Building Skill — Current Focus

Fraction Arithmetic Foundation

When we began adding fractions, PJ initially made the common mistake of adding denominators directly. After some targeted practice, we paused to focus on finding and using the Least Common Denominator (LCD).

We practiced the guiding principle:

"How many times can the old denominator go into the new one? Multiply that by the old numerator to get the new numerator."

By the end of the session, PJ was demonstrating growing confidence with this conversion step and correctly applying it to solve fraction problems.

🎯 Summary and Next Steps

PJ is learning quickly and showing steady improvement in both speed and conceptual understanding. Our next goal is to solidify the LCD process so it becomes automatic—allowing him to focus on higher-level reasoning during fraction and algebra problems.

He's on track for mastery. Great work, PJ!
