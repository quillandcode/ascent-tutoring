💡 The Critical Discovery: Cognitive Load

Today, we identified a pattern that's vital to understand: PJ is showing great promise with high-level algebra concepts. He understands how algebra works. The logic makes sense to him. But here's what's happening:

The "Slipping" Phenomenon

When PJ's brain is focusing intensely on new, complex concepts (like distributing or combining terms), the "old stuff"—basic arithmetic—tends to slip.

Specifically, he struggles with subtraction where a larger number is subtracted from a smaller one, resulting in a negative answer. This is completely normal. His brain is allocating all its energy to the algebra logic, leaving less mental bandwidth for the arithmetic underneath.

Think of it like a computer: when you're running a resource-intensive program, other processes slow down. PJ's "processor" is working overtime on the new algebra skills, so the arithmetic calculations get fewer resources. This doesn't mean he doesn't know his arithmetic—it means his brain is prioritizing the harder task.

🎯 The Number Line Strategy

To combat the cognitive load issue, we introduced a game-changing tool today: The Number Line Sketch.

Here's how it works: whenever PJ faces a subtraction problem involving negatives—especially when the result will be negative—he draws a quick number line on his scratch paper. This visual aid offloads the mental work from his brain to the paper, ensuring he doesn't make small calculation errors that could cost him points on exams.
