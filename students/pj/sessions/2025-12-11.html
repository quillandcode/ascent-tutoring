Equations & Inequalities
⚖️ Session Overview

Today's session was about something profound: making the invisible visible. PJ isn't learning algebra for the first time—he already understands it. What we're working on is something more challenging: retraining his brain to write down what it's already solving in his head.

Think of it like this: PJ has been running calculations in his mind like a high-speed processor. That's impressive. But his teacher needs to see the process, not just the answer. So we're teaching the mental mathematician to become a paper-trail architect—to document his thinking, step by step, line by line.

We focused on two critical principles: (1) Operations must be reversed when you move a term to the other side of the equal sign (addition becomes subtraction, multiplication becomes division), and (2) Whatever you do to one side must be done to the other side to keep the equation true. These aren't poetic ideas—they're the fundamental rules that make algebra work.

Today's mission: Solving Equations and Inequalities, with a special focus on the discipline of showing work—not because he doesn't know how, but because his teacher requires proof of the journey.

1
Verification (The Concept of Truth)
PJ immediately grasped how to check whether a value makes an equation or inequality "true." This is foundational work—understanding what we're even looking for. He sailed through this with zero friction. When you understand the why behind the math, the how becomes much easier. Status: Mastered.
2
One-Step Mechanics & The "Flip"

This is where the habit retraining began. PJ understands the mechanics—addition becomes subtraction, multiplication becomes division. But we focused heavily on writing down every step, even when his brain wants to skip ahead.

We spent targeted time on problems like x ÷ 11 = 3, reinforcing that we must multiply both sides by 11 to isolate x. The mechanics? Solid. The documentation? That's the new muscle we're building.

The Flip Trap: Here's the recurring challenge—remembering to flip the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number. This rule is counterintuitive; it goes against what our brains expect. PJ needs guided reminders to catch this specific rule during the solving process. This challenge keeps reappearing, but that's okay. Every time it shows up, it's another opportunity to strengthen that mental pathway.

Status: Habit Re-training & Procedural Focus.
3
Synthesizing Skills (The Two-Step)
Now we're combining tools. PJ successfully synthesized three major components: conceptual understanding, reversing operations, and applying the Flip rule when needed. This is where the ladder gets steeper, but he handled it well.

More importantly, he's building the habit of rewriting the entire equation with every step. This "line-by-line" approach ensures he doesn't lose track of the math—and it gives his teacher exactly what they're looking for: visible proof of logical progression.

Status: Solid Integration.

💡 The Critical Discovery

Here's what's really happening in PJ's algebra journey: He's not struggling with understanding—he's undergoing a transformation of habit.

Because his teacher acts as a facilitator rather than a direct lecturer, PJ is responsible for teaching himself via the packet. This means he needs to be able to decode instructions, apply concepts, and—critically—show his work in a way that proves he knows what he's doing.

Our Skill Ladder approach is helping him break down those packet instructions into manageable rungs. Each session, we're not just teaching algebra—we're teaching him how to learn algebra independently, how to document his thinking, and how to meet his teacher's expectations for visible work.

PJ has been a brilliant mental mathematician—solving problems in his head like an architect visualizing a building. But now, he needs to produce the blueprints. The building is still the same; we're just making sure he can draw the plans on paper so others can see his genius at work.

This isn't about fixing something broken. This is about adaptation—learning to communicate mathematics in the language his teacher requires. And he's doing it.

🎯 The Line-by-Line Strategy

To build the habit of visible work, we're reinforcing a critical practice: rewrite the entire equation with every step.

This accomplishes three things:

Clarity: It keeps PJ from losing track of where he is in the problem.

Proof: It gives his teacher the visible evidence they need to award full credit.

Habit: It trains his brain to default to documentation, not just calculation.
