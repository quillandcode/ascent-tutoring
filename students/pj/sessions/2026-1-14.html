🪜 The Skill Ladder
1
Circle Anatomy (Foundation)

We started at the beginning: What are the parts of a circle?

PJ learned to identify and calculate:

Radius (r): The distance from the center to the edge

Diameter (d): The distance across the circle through the center

The Relationship: d = 2r and r = d/2

This foundation was rock-solid. PJ understood not just the definitions, but how these parts connect. When you know the diameter, you can find the radius. When you know the radius, you can find the diameter. Simple, elegant, mastered.

Status: Mastered.
2
Circumference (Measuring the Edge)

Rung 2 introduced the concept of circumference—the distance around the circle. Think of it as the perimeter, but for circles.

C = 2πr
C = πd

We committed this formula to memory, understanding that π (pi) is approximately 3.14, and that circumference depends on either the radius or the diameter.

PJ worked through several problems, calculating circumference with precision. He understood the why behind the formula and applied it flawlessly. No hesitation. No errors. Just clean execution.

Status: Mastered.
3
Area (Measuring the Space Inside)

Now we moved from the edge to the interior: What's the area of a circle?

A = πr²

This is where the radius becomes critical. Area depends on the radius squared, meaning small changes in radius create big changes in area. PJ grasped this concept immediately.

We practiced multiple problems, calculating area with different radius values. PJ's simple math skills shined here—squaring numbers, multiplying by π, arriving at answers with confidence.

He also demonstrated excellent formula recall. When asked for the area formula, he didn't hesitate. When asked for circumference, same result. The knowledge was locked in.

Status: Mastered.
4
Real-World Application (Top of the Ladder)

This is where theory meets reality. PJ tackled word problems and real-world scenarios, extracting the known information, selecting the correct formula, and solving for the unknown.

📐 Scenario 1: The Circular Room

You need to lay carpet in a circular room. The room has a diameter of 20 feet. How much carpet do you need?

Solution: Find the area. Use A = πr². First, find the radius (10 feet), then calculate the area. Result: approximately 314 square feet of carpet needed.

🛣️ Scenario 2: The Circular Road

A circular piece of land has a diameter of 500 meters. You want to pave a road around the edge. How long will the road be?

Solution: Find the circumference. Use C = πd. Calculate: C ≈ 1,570 meters of paving material needed.

PJ handled these scenarios with remarkable clarity. He knew which formula to use. He identified the known values from the text. He substituted correctly. He solved confidently.

This is the highest level of understanding—taking abstract formulas and applying them to solve real problems. And PJ did it without breaking a sweat.

Status: Complete Mastery.
