🪜 The Skill Ladder
1
Coordinate Logic & Point Discovery

The foundation: A linear formula lets you find one coordinate if you have the other. Given a value for x, you can solve for y. This is the most basic relationship between equations and graphs.

We started by explaining how the formula connects to the line on a graph. Then I assigned problems where PJ needed to substitute a given x-value and solve for y.

⚠️ The First Hurdle

PJ initially entered "over-analyzing mode." When asked to solve for variables, he seemed to overthink the task, trying to find complexity where there was simplicity. This is a pattern I've seen before—when uncertainty strikes, he adds layers of unnecessary logic instead of trusting the straightforward process.

The Breakthrough: After I clarified the objective—"You're just plugging in the x-value and solving for y"—PJ's mental fog cleared. He proceeded to solve the remaining problems independently and successfully. Once he understood what was being asked, execution became automatic.

Status: Mastered after clarification.
2
The Slope Formula (m)

Rung 2 introduced slope—the rate of change of a line, calculated with the formula:

m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)

This formula had been introduced in a previous session, so PJ had some familiarity. The goal today was to drill the mechanics until they became second nature.

🔧 Execution Errors

PJ demonstrated conceptual understanding but struggled with mechanical accuracy. Common errors included:

• Mixing up coordinate pairs: Confusing y₁ with y₂, or mismatching x and y values from the same point.

• Subtracting negative numbers incorrectly: Forgetting that subtracting a negative becomes addition (e.g., 5 - (-3) = 8).

• Formula recall issues: Initially had trouble remembering which difference goes in the numerator versus the denominator.

We continued drilling problems until PJ reached a satisfactory level of accuracy. By the end of this rung, he could calculate slope reliably—though it required focused attention and double-checking his work.

Status: Functional competence achieved through repetition.
3
The y-intercept (b)

Rung 3 focused on defining b—the y-intercept. This is the specific point where the line crosses the y-axis, which occurs when x = 0.

We used Desmos to visualize linear equations. I showed PJ how changing the value of b shifts the line up or down on the graph. A positive b moves the line upward. A negative b moves it downward. The slope (m) stays the same—only the vertical position changes.

Then we moved to algebraic problems: given m and b, calculate y for a specific x-value. This is just substitution into the equation y = mx + b.

🧠 Mental Block Encountered

When asked to calculate y algebraically using the formula, PJ experienced a mental block. He seemed unsure how to proceed, even though the task was straightforward algebra—substituting values and solving.

The Breakthrough: Once I reframed the task as "simple algebra / solving for a variable," PJ's confusion dissolved. He recognized it as something he already knew how to do. From that point, he solved the problems with minimal guidance.

This rung reinforced an important insight: PJ sometimes needs tasks to be explicitly labeled or contextualized before he recognizes them as familiar territory. Once that connection is made, he executes confidently.

Status: Mastered after reframing.
